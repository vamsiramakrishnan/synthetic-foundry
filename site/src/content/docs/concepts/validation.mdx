---
title: Validation
description: What worldloom validate checks, and what it refuses to warn about.
---

```bash
worldloom validate ./corpus
```

The validator checks that the whole corpus agrees with itself. It prints the
number of checks it ran: 1,283 on the bundled `retail-close` example, 8,877 on
a rendered seed-8128 build, tens of thousands on a large world. Every failure
is a defect, not a warning.

## What it treats as a defect

- A total that does not equal the sum of its parts.
- A variance that is not actual less budget.
- A percentage that does not match the amounts it describes.
- A document citing a fact that did not yet exist when it was written.
- A reference to an entity, event, or fact that does not exist.
- A reporting line that cycles, or a service that owns itself.
- An author who cannot see the document they wrote.
- An approver who does not exist, wrote the document themselves, or is not
  permitted by the document's own access policy.
- Lore that constrains nothing.
- A fact a document was asked to carry and does not carry.
- A table cell that names a fact and states nothing.
- Fewer compiled documents than the plan asked for.

The last three are one rule seen from three sides. The worst defect this
project has had passed everything above them: a workbook looked its figures up
at the wrong month, rendered with every cell empty, and validated clean. A
reconciliation check compares a cell against a fact, and two absent numbers
agree. The validator now compares each intent's `required_fact_ids` against
its own compiled `ArtifactIR`, per document. That check found four more
defects of the same shape on its first run.

## Violations versus advisories

Some findings are true and are not coherence violations. The validator prints
them as readings, not counted against the verdict. The build above ends with:

```text
✓ coherent: 8861 checks passed

organisation: 3 reading(s), not counted against coherence
  person_reaches_nothing CO-0001: 9 of 23 person(s) are named as the subject
  of no fact that any compiled document carries on its readable surface ...
```

That is `validate.reachability`. An entity is *reached* when a fact names it
and a compiled document carries that fact on its readable surface. A declared
entity that nothing reaches is real thinness, but it is not incoherence, so it
does not fail the gate. It ships as a ratchet instead: an entity kind the
reading names is a knob that will not turn.

Coherence and thinness are different properties. A 58-fact bank once passed
validation cleanly while mentioning none of its 133 branches in any document.
Reachability is the reading that catches that; see
[Verticals](/worldloom/reference/verticals/) for what closing it did to corpus volume.

## After rendering

Run `validate` after every render, not only after narration. Rendering adds
its own checks:

- Every file the manifest names exists on disk.
- A chart cannot double-count: plotting a `SUM` cell beside its own operands
  draws the same money twice.
- Every number in a rendered document traces to a resolved `{{fact:...}}`
  reference.

## Do not negotiate with it

If you are tempted to make a check pass by editing the fixture or relaxing the
check: don't. A validator that can be talked out of failing is decoration. Fix
the thing it caught.
