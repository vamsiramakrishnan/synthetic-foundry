title: Authoring a company

Several surfaces answer "what kind of company is this?". This page orders them
from the cheapest to the most expressive. Each one is a strict no-op when
omitted, and each rides the recipe, so an authored corpus rebuilds itself.

## Archetypes: the stock shapes

```bash
worldloom archetypes
worldloom build --seed 8128 --archetype midsize_adi --out ./bank
```

Six ship: `australian_grocery`, `customer_owned_bank`, `midsize_adi`,
`midsize_general_insurer`, `midsize_infrastructure_services`, and
`omnichannel_retailer` (the default). An archetype fixes the company shape:
business units, categories, sites, headcount. Which episode runs on it is the
[vertical's](/worldloom/reference/verticals/) decision.

## Facets: one attribute at a time

```bash
worldloom pack facets
worldloom build --facet listing=listed --facet maturity=legacy --seed 8128 --out ./corpus
```

A facet says what the company *is*, as consequences into vocabularies that
already exist. `listing=listed` mints an audit committee chair and a head of
investor relations, raises status-report density, and puts the audit committee
in the filing approval chain. Two behaviours to know before you use one:

- Naming any facet settles every facet at its registry default. So
  `--facet listing=listed` alone also asserts `trading_pattern=steady`, a flat
  year. Name `trading_pattern=christmas_peak` to keep the December peak.
- Contradictory claims are refused naming both, with the arithmetic. A mutual
  runs 16–26% margin and a premium brand 48–62%; no company is both.

The recipe records the consequences, never the facet names. What a facet
implies and nothing implements is printed as `unmet:` rather than dropped.

## Locales: where the corpus is

```bash
worldloom pack locales australia
worldloom build --locale germany --seed 8128 --out ./corpus
```

Four ship: `australia`, `germany`, `gulf`, `united_kingdom`. A locale reaches
the figure grammar corpus-wide (Germany prints `1.234,50` and `-1.234`) and the
build: region labels in site names, name pools, headquarters, currency, fiscal
year, and the working week. The Gulf week runs Sunday to Thursday, and
every close date steps on the corpus's own calendar.

## Specifications: the company in one document

```bash
worldloom pack spec --template
worldloom build --spec company.json --seed 8128 --out ./corpus
```

A specification is the sentence "a listed mid-size German insurer, thin
margins, fragmented market" as one JSON document: industry, geo, facets,
organisation, leadership, identity. It is a composer, not an engine. Every
field resolves into a seam the flags already reach. What it adds is that the
pieces resolve together:

- It refuses a self-contradictory description, with the arithmetic. Revenue
  that cannot fit the headcount is refused naming both numbers and the
  registered shapes that bound them.
- It reports what it cannot honour rather than dropping it, on the same
  `unmet:` channel facets use.
- It is never recorded. It resolves to consequences and the recipe records
  those.

`--spec` refuses the flags it subsumes (`--archetype`, `--pack`,
`--employees`, `--facet`, `--physics`, `--locale`, `--estate`) rather than
merging with them. `--seed`, `--periods`, and `--incident` still apply: the
specification says what the company is, those say what happens to it.

## Packs: identity

```bash
worldloom pack template retail
worldloom pack check pack.json
worldloom build --pack pack.json --seed 8128 --out ./corpus
```

A pack is identity: the company's name, its units and their books, its lore,
its voices. A specification describes a class of businesses; a pack names one.
The pack embeds in the recipe verbatim, because the pack is how the world was
made. A pack's own name pools, regions, and headquarters win over a locale's.

## Physics: the ranges behind the figures

```bash
worldloom pack params retail
worldloom build --physics physics.json --seed 8128 --out ./corpus
```

A pack supplies values; the engine owns the ranges figures are drawn from.
`--physics` overrides those ranges from a JSON file. Writing one by hand is
the wrong instrument: the ranges are not independent, so derive it instead.

```bash
worldloom probe open -p "A field-services business, 900 people, four regions."
worldloom probe next probe.json
worldloom probe accept probe.json --from answer.json
worldloom probe resolve probe.json -o physics.json
```

The probe descends organisation → reporting → roles → objectives → measures,
one question at a time. You may narrow a bound and never widen it. Every
relation is invertible, so the graph is narrowed to arc consistency after each
answer; an answer that empties a range is refused naming the chain that broke.
`probe next` exits 3 when nothing is left to ask.

## Making the company bigger

`organisation.divisions` is the field that makes a corpus bigger, not
headcount. Raising headcount from 23 to 429 left facts, artifacts, and
evaluation cases unchanged, because 429 people still managed three divisions.
Widening the same retailer from three divisions to eight took facts from 604
to 990 on the same seed. Volume follows structure, because the close fans out
per division. `--policies core`, `--hiring`, and `--reviews` add the standing
documents and the line-management paperwork a company has rather than
produces.
