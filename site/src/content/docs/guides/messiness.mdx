---
title: Messiness and noise
description: Content messiness and filesystem noise are different failures.
---

A corpus that is perfectly kept has never tested recency or provenance
reasoning. Worldloom makes a corpus imperfect in two separate, recorded ways.
Content messiness lives in the documents. Filesystem noise lives in the drive
layout. They fail differently, and a realistic archive wants both.

## Content messiness: `build --messiness`

```bash
worldloom pack messiness
worldloom build --seed 8128 --messiness lived_in --out ./corpus
```

Four profiles grade how well the archive is kept. Each profile is a budget of
three imperfection kinds:

| Profile | Staleness | Disagreement | Orphaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `pristine` (default) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `well_run` | low | low | low |
| `lived_in` | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| `neglected` | 5 | 4 | 8 |

The three kinds: a document that missed a correction it postdates, two live
documents disagreeing with a ledger that says which is right, and an author
who has left with nobody named in their place.

One invariant does not relax: no document may contradict the ledger. Every
imperfection is recorded in `intentional-errors.jsonl`, so a reader holding
only the corpus can establish mechanically that the stale page is stale and
what the current position is. That record is what keeps this a corpus rather
than noise.

### Delivered versus asked

The counts are a budget, not a quota. A small world has fewer corrections to
be stale about, so the pass takes what the world supports and reports what it
delivered against what the profile asked. Read the build summary's
"labelled imperfections" line rather than assuming the budget landed in full.

## Filesystem noise: `workspace --noise`

```bash
worldloom workspace ./corpus -o ./drive --noise neglected
```

This makes the drive untidy the way real drives are: `Copy of X`, a document
dragged into `Shared/` or `_Inbox/`, somebody's `X FINAL` beside the real one,
an `_Archive/` leftover.

Every extra file is a byte-identical copy of real corpus content, never
invented text. A drive's junk is not fabricated documents. It is the same
documents saved again in the wrong place under the wrong name. That is what
makes it hard: a retriever cannot tell the copy from the original by reading
it. A copy carries the permissions of what it copies, so a misfiling lands
where nobody would look and stays readable only by the original's readers.

Every junk file is labelled in `permissions.jsonl` with its kind and what it
duplicates. The label is the whole difference between this and making a mess.
A benchmark scored against a drive it cannot account for cannot tell "the
assistant found the wrong copy" from "the assistant was wrong". Those are
different failures.

## Which one you want

- Testing recency and provenance reasoning: `--messiness`.
- Testing retrieval against a realistic drive, and access control:
  `workspace --noise`.
- A realistic archive benchmark: both.

Content messiness changes what documents say, so it is a build flag and rides
the recipe. Filesystem noise changes where copies sit, so it is a workspace
flag and touches the corpus not at all.
