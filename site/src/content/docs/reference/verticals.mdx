A vertical owns a causal episode: its fact kinds, its documents, its
invariants, and its benchmark. A [pack](/synthetic-foundry/guides/authoring/) changes the
company running an existing episode. A new vertical changes what happens.

## The four engines

| Vertical | Episode | What it models |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail | `MonthEndClose` | Financial reconciliation, operational incidents, multi-period histories, workforce and estate trajectories. |
| Banking | `QuarterlyCapitalReturn` | Second-line challenge, equal-authority conflict, filing under a lodgement norm, restatement. |
| Insurance | `QuarterlyReserving` | Triangle development, emergence, held versus central estimates, authority-sensitive answers. |
| Procurement | `PurchaseToPayCycle` | Purchase-to-pay controls, three-way matching, exceptions, carried shortfalls. |

The banking episode is worth spelling out. A capital return is challenged by
the second line, filed anyway, invalidated by a reconciliation break the daily
liquidity cadence catches, and corrected by a restatement: a new lodgement
that leaves the original on the record. Restatement is the one relationship
`revises` and `supersedes` both may not express.

## What each vertical measures

Each vertical measures what its own vocabulary owns: deposits, lending, and
front-line FTE by branch; written premium by underwriting office and claims by
claims centre; spend, commitment, and materials by depot, project office, and
yard. Every split goes through a largest-remainder allocator, so roll-ups
reconcile exactly, and a rate is never summed.

## Where volume comes from

Volume scales with the estate, not with a multiplier. Three of the four
verticals once declared full organisations that no fact named: a bank with
133 branches mentioned none of them, and its corpus was 58 facts. Every one of
those corpora passed validation, because coherence and thinness are different
properties. Closing the [reachability](/synthetic-foundry/concepts/validation/) reading produced
the volume:

| Vertical | Facts, one period | Documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retail | 588 | 7 |
| Banking | 58 → 744 | 11 → 12 |
| Insurance | 62 → 219 | 4 → 8 |
| Procurement | 52 → 217 | 6 → 7 |

Counts move with flags. A retail build with `--incident` mints 615 facts and
16 documents. A banking build at `--periods 3` mints 2,220 facts, verified:

```bash
worldloom build --seed 8128 --archetype midsize_adi --periods 3 --out ./bank3
wc -l bank3/facts.jsonl
# 2220
```

## Period support is explicit

- **Retail** runs multi-period histories with workforce and estate
  trajectories: `--periods`, `--timeline`, `--headcount-end`, `--sites-end`.
- **Banking and procurement** run consecutive periods, each episode chained
  onto the last, without retail's trajectories. Banking steps three months at
  a time.
- **Insurance** declares `max_periods=1`. A second consecutive valuation
  quarter is phase two of that engine. `--periods 2` is refused at plan time,
  naming the cap, rather than building a world and failing inside the episode.

No flag is silently ignored to simulate a history a vertical does not
implement. Retail-only flags (`--incident`, `--comparatives`, `--actors`) are
refused on the other engines rather than dropped.

## Choosing one

```bash
worldloom archetypes
worldloom build --seed 8128 --out ./retail
worldloom build --seed 8128 --archetype midsize_adi --out ./bank
worldloom build --seed 8128 --archetype midsize_general_insurer --out ./insurer
```

The archetype picks the company shape, and the shape picks the engine. For an
industry none of the four models, author an industry pack over the nearest
engine, or register a new vertical through the extension seams. The
`worldloom-vertical` skill in the repository walks that path.
